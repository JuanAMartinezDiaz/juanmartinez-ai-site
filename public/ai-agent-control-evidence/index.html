Can Your Board Prove Its AI Agents Are Under Control?

Policies document intentions. Evidence demonstrates whether autonomous agents operate within approved boundaries. This six-domain diagnostic helps boards, risk committees and second-line oversight teams identify where assurance is demonstrable—and where it remains an assertion.

By Juan A. Martinez Diaz, MBA · Updated 15 October 2026

The governance-to-evidence gap

Agentic systems can select tools, initiate transactions, and act across applications. An approved AI policy alone cannot establish whether an agent's actual permissions, actions and exceptions remain within risk appetite. The board needs traceable evidence of control design, operating effectiveness and independent challenge.

Ask management to show the population of agents, their accountable owners, their granted authority, rejected actions, incident escalation and revocation test results. If evidence cannot be produced, the appropriate conclusion is not demonstrated—not necessarily that the control has failed.

Rate each domain using documented evidence, not verbal assurances. No confidential details are uploaded; calculations occur in your browser.

How to interpret the result

0 = no evidence; 1 = policy or design only; 2 = implemented with artifacts; 3 = sampled operating effectiveness; 4 = independently tested and monitored. Scores are an original educational diagnostic, not an official NIST rating, certification, or validated predictor of losses. A high total cannot compensate for critical unmitigated deficiencies.

Board reporting: what good looks like

Provide a reconciled agent inventory and coverage denominator; an owner and authorized purpose for each agent; permission-to-action traces; allow/deny logs; exceptions with age and risk acceptance; kill-switch tests; and independent assurance findings with remediation status. Trend these measures over time and disclose known coverage gaps.
